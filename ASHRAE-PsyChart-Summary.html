The key tension

55 and 62.1 pull toward more conditioning and ventilation; 90.1 pulls toward less energy. Guideline 36 is the rulebook that reconciles them — economizer changeover, demand-controlled ventilation, supply-air-temp and duct-static resets, trim-and-respond.

How the Psy-chart approach satisfies all four

one decision surfaceT × humidity / enthalpy

All four standards share the same axes — dry-bulb temperature × humidity/enthalpy — so one plotted point answers all four questions simultaneously.

StandardWhat the Psy-chart shows / does
55 — ComfortThe comfort zone is drawn directly on the chart. Plot the room condition → instantly see if you're inside the acceptable T/RH band, and which way you're off (too humid, too cold).
62.1 — VentilationThe mixed-air point sits on the line between return and outdoor air; its position = the OA fraction. The chart makes the actual OA ratio visible, and CO₂/DCV overlays confirm the minimum fresh-air quantity.
90.1 — EnergyEnthalpy lines reveal free cooling: when OA enthalpy is below return, the economizer meets the load with zero mechanical cooling. The shortest path from mixed-air to supply-air point = least energy.
G36 — SequencesThe chart is visual proof the sequences (economizer changeover, SAT reset, DCV, dehumidification staging) land the operating point inside the 55 comfort zone, on the 62.1 OA line, hugging the 90.1 low-enthalpy path.
In one sentence

The psychrometric chart turns four separate rulebooks into a single decision surface — plot where the air is, see where it must be (comfort zone), how it gets there (mixing & coil process lines), and whether it does so at minimum energy (enthalpy) — which is exactly the judgment Guideline 36 automates in software.
