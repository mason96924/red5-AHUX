Humidity ratio W vs absolute humidity

Both measure “how much water is in the air,” but they use different denominators. Red5-AHU charts and mixing diagnostics use humidity ratio W, not volumetric absolute humidity.

W · kg/kg · g/kg Absolute · g/m³ Lever rule / mixing

One-line difference

W is water mass per mass of dry air. Absolute humidity (typical usage) is water mass per volume of moist air.

W — humidity ratio

W = m_vapor / m_dry_air

≈ 0.621945 · p_w / (P − p_w)
  • Vertical axis on the psychrometric chart
  • Basis for OA–RA–MA off-chord checks
  • Conserved across adiabatic mixing of dry-air streams

Absolute humidity

AH ≈ m_vapor / V_moist

≈ ρ_moist · (W / (1 + W))
  • Common in weather / IAQ “grams per cubic metre”
  • Changes when air is heated or cooled at constant W
  • Not the quantity used for AHU mixing geometry

Why psych charts and AHU mixing use W

Outdoor air + return air mixing is a mass balance on dry air. Humidity ratio W is the moisture variable that stays on the straight OA–RA chord (lever rule). Absolute humidity in g/m³ shifts with temperature even when the moisture mass ratio is unchanged.

That is why red5-ahu stores w on each psy point (OA, SA, RA, MA) and why “off the mixing line” is measured in g/kg of humidity ratio, not g/m³.

Intuition

Same moist air cooled at constant W → absolute humidity (g/m³) usually rises, because the same vapor sits in a smaller volume. W is unchanged until condensation starts.

Same air heated at constant W → absolute humidity (g/m³) usually falls; W still unchanged.

In red5-ahu

  • getW(t, rh) → humidity ratio in kg/kg
  • Chart / badges display moisture as g/kg (×1000)
  • MA diagnostics (mixed_air.py) use W for the OA–RA chord and line_deviation_g_kg
