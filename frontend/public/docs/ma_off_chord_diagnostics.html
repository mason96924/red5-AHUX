When MA leaves the OA–RA line

If mixed air does not sit on the outdoor–return chord, the deviation is a fault signal — economizer / damper health, HRV–ERV transfer, duct leakage, or sensors. This page breaks down what can (and cannot) be identified from that geometry.

OA RA MA SA Lever rule APAR-aligned

Physics in one sentence

Ideal adiabatic mixing conserves dry-air mass and moisture, so a measured MA state (MAT + humidity) must lie on the straight OA–RA segment and divide it in the ratio of the two mass flows (lever rule). Dry-bulb is linear to ~0.05 K — invisible at chart scale.

Why the chord matters

OA→SA is not one physical process — it spans mixing then the coil. With MA present: OA/RA→MA = mixing box; MA→SA = coil(s); SA→RA = room load.

Hypothesis ranking (when MA is off-chord)

One geometry cannot uniquely name the cause. Rank hypotheses with OAD, and with ERV status when the plant has a wheel.

# Pattern Likely cause Why Needs
A Off-chord + small damper_mismatch Sensor / probe (MAT or MAH) Mix fraction matches OAD; moisture doesn’t match T-lever MAT+MAH, OAD
B Off-chord + large damper_mismatch Economizer / damper / leakage / stratification Commanded OA% ≠ actual mix fraction MAT, OAD (± MAH)
C mat_outside_oa_ra (f < 0 or > 1) Sensor bias or stratification MAT outside OA–RA temperature range (APAR Rule 10 family) MAT, OA, RA, ΔT ≥ ~2 °C
D Off-chord wetter than mix; OAD ≈ minimum Unmeasured moisture / EA path / duct leak Extra water mass not explained by OA–RA mix MAT+MAH
E Plotted OA ≠ air entering the mixing box ERV / HRV Wheel moves OA toward RA in h (and sometimes w) before mixing EA T/RH, wheel enable, or OA-after-ERV
F Off-chord only when wheel ON; gone when OFF ERV / HRV Controlled A/B confirms transfer, not sensor Wheel status + same sensors
G Off-chord mainly at high OAD / economizer high Economizer leakage / nonlinear damper / bypass Mixing imperfect at extreme positions OAD + MAT(+MAH)
H Persistent bias independent of OAD & wheel Sensor drift (OA, RA, or MA) Not explained by process mode Cross-check / handheld

Important clarifications

  • A healthy economizer moves MA along the chord (change foa). Off-chord under economizer → leakage, stratification, or bad RH — not “economizer on” by itself.
  • ERV/HRV often means free-stream OA on the chart is not the OA that mixed. Prefer parents OApost-ERV–RA, or treat free-stream OA–RA off-chord as an ERV signature when the wheel is enabled.
  • A single-point MAT in a mixing box often reads stratification, not a fully mixed average — the most common false damper-mismatch.

Decision tree

Have MAT + MAH?
  No  → damper vs f_t only (economizer health) — NOT off-chord ID
  Yes → deviation_gkg + f_t vs f_w vs f_damper
        │
        ├─ |f_t − f_w| large          → MAH or MAT suspect (or stratification)
        ├─ |f − OAD| large, on-chord  → economizer / damper / leakage
        ├─ off-chord + ERV enable     → ERV/HRV (confirm EA or OA-after-wheel)
        ├─ off-chord, ERV off, OAD ok → sensor / duct moisture / leak
        └─ MAT outside OA–RA range    → sensor / stratification first

Signals required for confidence

Question Minimum Strong
Is mix ratio matching the damper? MAT + OAD + MAH (ft vs fw)
Is MA thermodynamically on the chord? MAT + MAH + stable ΔT(OA,RA) ≥ 2 °C
Economizer vs sensor? Above + OAD Trend vs OAD; handheld check
ERV/HRV vs everything else? Wheel enable OA after wheel, or EA T/RH

Bottom line

Yes — we can break off-chord MA into a hypothesis ranking (sensor · economizer/damper · ERV/HRV · leak), not a single guaranteed label from geometry alone.

With MAT+MAH+OAD you get a solid first pass. With ERV status (or post-ERV OA) you can separate the wheel from sensor/economizer. Next product step: a classifier on mixing that emits likely: sensor | economizer | erv | leak with confidence.
